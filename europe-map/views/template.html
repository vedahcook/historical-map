City views for the Europe map

A working mockup of the new city popup. Pick a city and move the year: the picture is the view of the city made closest to that year, and the tabs show its events, who held it, and its population.

The popup

  • It keeps its size. Nothing in it grows or shrinks with the year, so the timeline stays put and you can tap one icon after another.
  • Three tabs. Events (shown first), Held by and Population each have a band on the same years, a box with details for the map’s year under it, and a list. Tap anything on a band or in a list to move the map to that year.
  • Events. The box shows the latest event up to the map’s year. On the event’s own year its icon turns blue.
  • Population. The list gives sudden rises and falls in the figures, with the events of those years that help explain them, such as plague, war, or the city taking in its suburbs.

The picture

  • Closest date. The picture is the view made closest to the map’s year, labeled with when it was made and how far that is from the map’s year. None if it would be more than 100 years away: an empty plate of the same size names the nearest ones.
  • Views of events. A view of an event, such as Cologne in ruins in 1945, is never shown for years before it.
  • Credit. The maker, the work and the license are at the bottom. Tap the picture for the whole view, uncropped.

On phones

  • The city opens as a full-width sheet from the bottom. Drag it down, or tap the bar at the top, to shrink it to the name and picture and see the map; drag down again to close. The ‹ › buttons next to the name change the year (hold to run).
