Europe's Borders, 1800–2026

Who held what on July 1 of every year, from OpenHistoricalMap checked against other datasets. Crosshatching marks occupied land; dashed outlines mark places where another source draws a different border.

How this map was made

Each year shows the borders in force on July 1. The map uses OpenHistoricalMap (OHM), which matched the historical record in 149 of 160 spot checks. Where OHM has no record, it falls back to CShapes-Europe (from 1816) or Cliopatria. Where our checks found OHM wrong, the map applies a correction and says so.

Occupations: OHM records named occupation regimes, such as the General Government in Poland or the Allied zones of Germany, and the map uses them. Where OHM shows the country that held the land in law, the map adds occupations from Cliopatria’s year-by-year outlines of each power’s control (1914-46), land annexed during the Second World War and given back afterward, and the breakaway regions and occupied parts of Ukraine since 1990. Each of these is listed under corrections.

Alternative borders compare OHM with CShapes-Europe from 1816 and with Cliopatria before that. They count only when a source puts an area of at least 400 km² (5,000 km² for the coarser Cliopatria) in a different country for longer than a year. Territories that governed themselves under an overlord count as part of the overlord for this comparison, because the sources disagree on whether to show them.

Cities appear when a country is highlighted: its largest cities that year (up to ten, and none smaller than 3% of its biggest), from about 390 cities (Europe's largest by 1900, and those that grew large after), placed in whichever country held them that year. Tap one for its population. Figures come from censuses where there is one for that year; between figures the map estimates, assuming steady growth, and says so. A hollow dot means there is no figure within ten years; the map then gives the nearest figures instead of an estimate. Some early figures are estimates for the wider city, so they are not always comparable with later census counts.
